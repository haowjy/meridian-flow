# Prologue

The Prologue of *The Long Reckoning*, which the landing page excerpts. The shared text runs to the ninth night; the card's choice keeps one of the four endings. The canon behind it is in [story.md](story.md).

The Cold Fire came on a clear night in spring, and every country saw it.

Four of us watched it from four high places, and none of us knew the others yet. In Harl a farm boy stood on the cliff above his father's barley and watched the north turn green. In Ansgard a dwarf stood on the watch-ledge of the Ninth Hold with a chisel still in her hand. At the edge of Vell an elf who had not seen anything in a thousand years lifted her face, because the wind had gone cold from the wrong direction. On the Kessa steppe a guide stood on a ridge with his horses and said nothing, because he knew the song, and the song had started.

It looked like a curtain. That was what everyone said afterward, in every language: a green curtain hung across the top of the world, moving slowly, as if someone behind it were breathing.

Every night it reached a little further south, and the sun came up a little colder.

By the tenth day the barley in Harl had stopped growing. By the twentieth there was frost on the river at noon. The scholars in every capital argued about it and agreed on one thing: whatever the Cold Fire was, it was eating the light. The sun still rose. It rose pale, like a coin seen through water, and gave off about as much heat.

The crowns sent armies first. Harl sent two thousand spears north under the king's own banner. Ansgard emptied a hold's garrison. Vell sent its rangers, and the Kessa, who have no armies, sent every hunter who would go. None of them came back. No runner, no message. The System still listed them, for those with the right to see such lists, by name and by level, and under every name the same word: *Missing*.

So they stopped sending armies.

Four peoples sent their strongest north to kill whatever was breathing it.

We were the four.

---

Tam came from Harl, the furthest south, so the road began with him. He was seventeen. Eighteen months earlier he had been a farm boy; now he was Level 58, higher than anyone his age had ever been, and when the king said so in front of the whole court Tam went red to the ears. His skill was [Spark]. The first one had come out of his right hand and taken the skin off to the elbow, and he still held that arm a little away from his body, as if it belonged to someone he was being polite to. He talked when he was nervous, which was most of the first week.

At the border fort under the mountains, Sigrun was waiting on the wall. She came down without being asked, looked at Tam's arm and said, "That'll ache in the cold." It did. She was the champion of the holds, a hundred and forty years old and built like a millstone, and both her forearms were scarred from wrist to elbow in neat rows of runes, the newest still scabbed. Tam asked about them that night. "A shield can be dropped," she said, which was not an answer until the wolves came. She carried a letter under the seal of the Ninth Hold and never opened it where anyone could see.

Maren was waiting at the edge of the forest, alone, with a staff, a pack, and the queen of Vell's ring on a cord around her neck. She was a thousand and forty years old and had been blind since she was six. Elves level slowly; it had taken her nine hundred years as the queen's healer to reach seventy. The System read her boxes to her aloud, in a voice she said sounded like her mother's. Nobody answered that. Nobody could think how.

The steppe was where they lost the road. The first snow came down sideways, and by noon Tam, Sigrun and Maren could not tell the track from the plain. Oskar rode out of it leading two spare horses. Nobody had told him they were lost. He was Kessa and bear-blooded, twice Sigrun's height, with half his left ear gone, and the clan mothers had sent him because he had walked into the ice and come back, which nobody else alive had done. When Tam asked about the ear, he said, "The ice." When Tam asked what in the ice, he said, "Eat your food." He believed the songs.

---

Nine days from the last Kessa camp to the place under the brightest part of the Cold Fire. Oskar said it like a fact, and it was one.

It got darker every day. The sun still came up and crossed the sky, but by the fourth day it gave no more light than a lamp behind a sheet, and the snow under it was the green of the sky above. It was cold enough to freeze Sigrun's beard to her collar. Tam's sparks came out of his hand blue. Maren walked with one hand on Oskar's pack strap and never stumbled. She said the snow sounded different over ice. Twice she told the others to step left, and both times there was a crevasse.

On the fifth day we found the army.

The men were sitting down. That was the worst of it. Two thousand men of Harl sat in the snow in their ranks with their spears across their knees, as though someone had called a halt and they had all sat down to wait. Frost had grown over them like fur. Over every one of them the System still hung a tag, small and patient: a name, a level, and the word *Missing*. It had not decided they were dead. Tam walked along the front rank reading names until Sigrun took him by the collar and walked him away.

They had not fought anything. They had just stopped.

On the sixth night the wolves came, twenty tags bobbing over the snow like lanterns. [Frost Wolf, Lv 41] Oskar was on his feet with his axe before anyone else had seen one. "Left," he said, "then behind," and they came from the left, and then from behind. That was his skill, [Sense Fear]: he heard an animal's fear a breath before it moved.

Sigrun stood in front of Maren, and the first wolf to reach her hit her raised forearm and stopped as if it had hit a wall. The rune under its teeth flared white and went dark. She was cutting the next one before the wolf had found its feet. [Ward Rune]: one blow per mark, then carve another. Tam understood about the shield.

Tam's sparks would not catch in that cold, so he fought with his knife, badly, and a wolf got its teeth into his scarred arm and held on. Then Maren sang. Nobody had heard her sing before. The bite closed around the wolf's teeth, the wolf let go as though it had bitten something wrong, and Sigrun killed it. [Mending Hymn]: while she sang, a wound closed.

It was the first time we had fought together, and we were not good at it. We were alive at the end. We decided that counted.

That night Oskar built a fire in the lee of a ridge, and while the meat cooked he sang the Kessa song. It is a long song, and most of it is about weather. The end is about a dragon that sleeps in the ice and wakes when the sky burns, and breathes the sky cold, and eats the sun.

Tam laughed, but kindly; he was seventeen and alive, and the wolves were dead. Sigrun said there were no dragons and never had been. There were no bones, and dwarves knew bones. Oskar did not argue. He turned the meat.

Maren did not laugh. She said, "Sing the last verse again," and he did.

Later, when the fire was low, Tam asked what everyone would do once it was dead. Sigrun said sleep for a month. Maren said she would go home and tell the queen she had been right, and would not say about what. Oskar said he would have to change the end of the song. Tam was going to buy his father the field next to theirs. He had already picked it out.

On the eighth day Maren stopped walking. She turned her face to the north and listened for a long time, and then she said, "It's very big," in a voice nobody had heard her use.

An hour later Tam saw it: far off over the ice, against the brightest part of the green, a shape turning slowly, too big and too slow to be a bird. He said it was a bird. Sigrun said it was cloud. Over every living thing they had met since Harl, the System had hung a tag, and Sigrun looked for this one's and found nothing.

Oskar had sung most days on the march. After that he stopped.

---

On the ninth night, we found it. It was a dragon.

The ice ended in a basin as wide as a city, and the beast lay in the middle of it with its head raised to the sky, breathing. Every breath went up green. That was the Cold Fire.

Oskar said nothing. Sigrun, who knew bones, said nothing at all.

We had a plan, and it had seemed a good one at the fort. Sigrun in front with her wards. Maren behind her, singing. Oskar on the flank, calling its moves. Tam behind them all, with his spark for its eyes.

It almost worked.

The first breath it turned on us, Sigrun took on her left arm. The rune flared white and the green fire split around us like water around a stone, and for a moment we stood in a pocket of warm air in the middle of all that cold, and Tam laughed out loud. Then the rune went dark, and Sigrun was already cutting the next one.

"Tail," Oskar called. "Down. Now." We went down, and the tail went over us with a sound like a ship's keel on gravel. "Left foot. It's afraid for its left foot." None of it made sense until Tam put a spark into the scales above its left foot and the beast flinched, the whole hill of it, and swung its head round. For one long breath its eye was in front of them, as big as a door and green inside, like the sky.

Tam put his spark into it.

It screamed. We had not known it could make a sound. The ice cracked under the noise in long white lines, the Cold Fire overhead flickered, and for one moment the sun showed through it, white and warm.

We had hurt it. We had actually hurt it.

Then it killed us.

---

## If Tam survives

It killed Sigrun first, because she was in front and the rune on her right arm was only half cut. Oskar shouted and went toward her, and the tail took him off his feet and into the dark. Maren was kneeling over Sigrun, singing, when the breath found her. She didn't stop singing until it was over.

Then there was only me on the ice, with one spark left and a right arm that would not close.

A box opened in the air in front of me. It was the same blue as every box I had ever seen, and it said:

**You died.**

I read it three times. I was still standing.

The beast turned its head toward the box, the brightest thing left on the ice. Its one eye narrowed, and it breathed. That's it? I was just getting good at this.

The fire went through the box and through me, colder than anything I have a word for.

I was on the cliff above my father's barley. It was night, and the north was turning green for the first time. Down in the farmhouse my mother was laughing at something.

## If Maren survives

It took Tam first. He was still standing where he had put the spark into its eye, still laughing, and the breath came down on him and the laughing stopped. Oskar was next, and I heard it, all of it. Sigrun stood over me with both arms raised and every rune on them dark, and said, "Sing," and I sang, and it was not enough.

Then there was only me on the ice.

Something was in front of me. I did not know what it was. I had not seen anything since I was six years old, and at first I did not understand that I was seeing. It was blue. It was shaped like nothing in the world, perfectly square, and it had words on it, and I could read them.

**You died.**

The System had read me ten thousand boxes in my mother's voice. It had never shown me one.

I had perhaps a breath to look at it before the beast turned its head toward the light. It breathed, and the blue went white.

So that's what fire looks like.

Then there was nothing to see again.

I was at the edge of Vell. The wind was cold from the wrong direction, and somewhere north of me, I knew, the sky was turning green.

I have told all of this as if I saw it. I didn't. Tam told me the sky was green. Sigrun told me about the tags. Everything in it with eyes in it, someone told me. The box is the only thing I saw myself.

The next one, the System read to me in my mother's voice.

## If Sigrun survives

It went for Maren first. I don't know how it knew she was the one keeping us standing, but it knew, and it went past me to reach her. Her song stopped in the middle of a word. Tam came at it from the left with his knife and his bad arm, shouting at it to look at him, and it did. Oskar was calling the next breath when it came. He was right about where it would land. He was standing in it.

Then there was only me on the ice, with the chisel in my hand and half a rune cut into my arm.

A box opened in the air in front of me.

**You died.**

I am a dwarf. I keep exact accounts, and I was still breathing. The System has never been wrong.

The beast swung its head round to the box, the brightest thing on the ice. I raised the arm with the half-cut rune, and it did nothing at all.

One more stroke and that rune would have held.

The fire came through the box and through me.

I was on the watch-ledge of the Ninth Hold, the chisel still in my hand. In the north the sky was turning green for the first time, and behind me my sister was calling up the stairs to ask if I was coming in to eat.

## If Oskar survives

I heard it decide. That is what [Sense Fear] gives you: you hear the fear, and right behind it you hear the choice. It was afraid of Tam's fire, so it killed Tam, who was still holding his burned arm out in front of him as if he could do it again. It was afraid of Sigrun's wards, so it went through them one at a time until there were none left on her arms, and then it went through her. Maren it killed because she would not stop singing.

It was not afraid of me. I was the one who knew the song.

Then there was only me on the ice.

A box opened in the air in front of me.

**You died.**

The song has no verse about boxes. I have sung it all my life, and I would know.

The beast turned its head. I heard the fear go out of it, and then I heard what it wanted instead: the light. The box was the brightest thing on the ice. It breathed on it the way it breathed on the sun, and I was standing under it.

The song was true, and there's no one left to sing it.

The fire took what the ice had left of my ear, and then the rest of me.

I was on the ridge with my horses. The north was turning green, and the song had started.
