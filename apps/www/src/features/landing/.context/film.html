How the landing film works

The scroll position is the clock. timeline.ts cuts the page into beats; every frame, each layer reads how far through each beat the visitor is and draws itself from that.

The timeline

Drag the playhead, or click a beat. Each beat has a scroll length in viewport heights (what the visitor swipes) and an autoplay length in seconds (what the play button spends on it). Ticks are the named cues inside a beat.

The layers

Back to front. Two canvases are fixed to the window; everything else sits on the sticky stage, stacked by the --z-* table on .cine .stage in film.css. The dragon changes layer as it flies: far behind the text, low over the trees, over the product, and in front of everything while it burns the box.

Changing it

  • A pause or a longer moment is a row in BEATS. Split a beat whose parts need retiming on their own, as the death is split into dies, still, flight and burn, instead of remapping shares inside it.
  • A moment two layers must agree on (when the dragon lands, when the party is gone) is a named cue in timeline.ts. A layer keeps only the curves nothing else matches.
  • Where text and the product sit is CSS, in container units of the stage; film-layout.ts reads it back. Only the scenery places itself in script.
  • Ground lines (the beach, the shore) are ground.ts. Colours are tokens: marketing.css, or marketing-sky.ts for the shaders.
  • Story copy is story.ts; read story.md before changing it.
